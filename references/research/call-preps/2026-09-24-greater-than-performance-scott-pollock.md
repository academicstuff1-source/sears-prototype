# 📞 Follow-Up Call Battlecard: Scott Pollock (Greater Than Performance)

**Goal:** Secure a 15-minute workflow mapping session.

---

## 1. The Context (10-Second Review)
- **Who:** Scott Pollock, Owner / Physical Therapist
- **Company:** Greater Than Performance
- **Target Demo:** Cash-based Physical Therapy clinic. Solo or boutique owner-operator.
- **The Angle:** "The Overwhelmed Owner-Operator." Cash-pay PT clinics typically have high patient engagement but suffer from manual intake forms, appointment reminders, and follow-ups. If he is solo, he's wearing both the clinician and admin hats. 
- **Current Status:** Second attempt. You called yesterday (Sept 23) and hit his voicemail, but didn't leave a message. 

## 2. Gatekeeper Strategy (Turning them into an Influencer)
If you hit an admin instead of Scott, position yourself as their ally. They block salespeople, but champion anyone who makes their job easier.

- **The Ask:** *"Hey there, this is Michael. Is Scott around, or is he in a session with a patient right now?"*
- **The Pivot:** *"No worries. I work with a few local cash-based clinics on automating patient intake and missed calls. As the one managing the front, I imagine it gets pretty chaotic for you trying to chase down intake forms and catch every ringing phone while Scott is tied up?"*
- **The Ask for Access:** *(If they agree)* *"That's exactly why I wanted to connect with him. What is the best way for me to catch him between sessions so I can show him how to take that intake paperwork completely off your plate?"*

## 3. The Script

### Phase 1: Pattern Interrupt & Permission
> *"Hey Scott, it's Michael. I know I'm catching you right in the middle of a busy clinic day — do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I'm calling, and then you can hang up if it's not relevant?"*

### Phase 2: The Peer Hook (Specificity & Pain)
> *"I work with other cash-based clinic owners in DFW. A massive problem they're facing right now is getting bogged down with manual intake forms, patient reminders, and chasing down leads after hours when they should be resting. It's usually costing them hours every week in manual admin or causing them to lose follow-ups."*

### Phase 3: The Pivot (No-Oriented Question)
> *"Would it be a ridiculous idea to ask how your clinic is currently handling that admin volume?"*

## 4. High-Yield Discovery Questions
*(If he opens up, ask these to widen the pain and surface the cost of inaction)*
1. *"When a new patient inquiry comes in at 8 PM, what exactly happens between then and 8 AM the next day?"*
2. *"How much of your week is spent just chasing people for missing paperwork or confirmations instead of treating patients?"*
3. *"If you did nothing about this for the next 12 months, what does that actually cost you in lost revenue or wasted time?"*

## 5. Objection Handling (Acknowledge & Pivot)

| Objection | The Play |
|---|---|
| **"We already have an EHR/software for that."** | *"That makes total sense. Most clinics I speak with use Jane or a similar EHR. We don't replace that—we build the 'glue' that stops you from having to manually move data in and out of it. How well is your system handling your after-hours follow-ups?"* |
| **"I am too busy to talk right now."** | *"I completely understand, and that’s exactly why I called. If you're slammed, it means manual admin is eating your margins. Would it be a terrible idea to put 15 minutes on the calendar next week when things cool down?"* |
| **"Send me an email."** | *"Happy to. So I don't send you generic spam, what is your biggest operational bottleneck right now: patient intake, or lead follow-up?"* |

## 6. The Close (Zero-Risk Diagnostic)
> *"I’d love to show you a 5-minute visual map of how a competitor solved this exact issue. The worst that happens is you get a free map of your workflow gaps to keep, and if it makes sense, we can talk about how to implement it. Do you have 15 minutes on [Day]?"*
