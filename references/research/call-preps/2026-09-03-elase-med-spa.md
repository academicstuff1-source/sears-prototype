# 📞 Cold Call Battlecard: VP of Operations / Regional Director at Elase Med Spa

**Goal:** Secure a 15-minute workflow mapping session.

---

## 1. The Context (10-Second Review)
- **Who:** VP of Operations or Regional Director for Texas
- **The Angle:** They are operating at corporate scale (7+ locations in Texas). At this size, the gap between Corporate Marketing (who generates the leads) and Local Front Desks (who have to call the leads) is huge. We pitch the "Speed-to-Lead" angle—automating the bridge between corporate ads and local booking.

## 2. The Script

### Phase 1: Pattern Interrupt & Permission
> *"Hey, it's Michael. I know I'm catching you right in the middle of your day — do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I'm calling, and then you can hang up if it's not relevant?"*

### Phase 2: The Peer Hook (Specificity & Pain)
> *"I work with other multi-location clinic operators here in Texas. A massive problem they face when they scale past 5 locations is the disconnect between corporate marketing and the local front desk. Corporate spends thousands generating ad leads, but local receptionists take 4 to 8 hours to call them back because they are busy checking in physical patients, which ends up torching the marketing ROI."*

### Phase 3: The Pivot (No-Oriented Question)
> *"Would it be a ridiculous idea to ask how you're currently ensuring leads generated by corporate are getting instantly triaged by the local clinics in Plano or Sugar Land?"*

## 3. High-Yield Discovery Questions
*(If they open up, ask these to widen the pain and surface the cost of inaction)*
1. *"When an ad lead comes in at 2 PM, does it get routed to a central call center, or does a local receptionist have to chase it down between checking in patients?"*
2. *"If a local clinic gets slammed on a Friday, what happens to the inbound leads that hit their specific queue?"*
3. *"If you are spending corporate budget on these ads, what does a 4-hour delay in local response time actually cost you in lost bookings across all 7 Texas locations?"*

## 4. Objection Handling (Acknowledge & Pivot)

| Objection | The Play |
|---|---|
| **"We use Zenoti/Enterprise Software for that."** | *"Zenoti is incredible for checking people in and handling memberships. But most clinics tell me it’s terrible at instantly pulling leads from Facebook ads and triaging them via two-way SMS before a human gets involved. I don't replace your EHR, I build the API glue that connects it to your ads. Are your current tools fully booking the patient automatically?"* |
| **"We have a centralized call center."** | *"That’s a huge structural advantage. But even call centers get backed up or go home at 6 PM. How much overflow or after-hours volume is hitting voicemail and bleeding out to competitors?"* |
| **"I'm too busy right now."** | *"I completely understand, and that’s exactly why I called. If you're slammed, it means operational friction is likely eating your bandwidth. Would it be a terrible idea to put 15 minutes on the calendar next week when things cool down?"* |

## 5. The Close (Zero-Risk Diagnostic)
> *"I’d love to show you a 5-minute visual map of how a similar multi-location clinic automated their inbound triage so their local front desks never had to manually chase an ad lead again. The worst that happens is you get a free map of your operational gaps to keep. Do you have 15 minutes on [Day]?"*
