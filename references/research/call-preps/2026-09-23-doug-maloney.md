# 📞 Cold Call Battlecard: Doug Maloney (First Texas Roofing)

**Goal:** Secure a 15-minute workflow mapping session.

---

## 1. The Context (10-Second Review)
- **Who:** Doug Maloney, Owner
- **The Angle:** He's an owner-operator who personally manages job sites and negotiates with insurance companies. He uses an `@att.net` email address, indicating low-tech operations. He likely loses massive revenue to missed calls when he's busy.
- **The Entry:** Pure Cold Call. You may hit a voicemail, Doug directly, or an Office Manager (often a spouse or solo admin).

## 2. Gatekeeper Strategy (If you hit an Office Manager)

### The Influencer Pivot
*Goal: Roofing office managers are usually stressed out chasing down crews, tracking insurance checks, and fielding inbound calls during storms. Acknowledge the chaos to make them a champion.*
- **The Ask:** *"Hey there, this is Michael. Is Doug around, or is he out on a roof?"* (Shows industry fluency).
- **The Pivot (If he's out):** *"No worries. To be honest, I'm working with a few local roofing owners on a system that intercepts those inbound quote requests when the team is out in the field. As the person holding down the fort, I imagine it gets pretty chaotic for you trying to route leads and track down guys in the field when it gets busy?"*
- **If they agree:** *"That's exactly why I wanted to pick Doug's brain. When is the best time for me to catch him so I can show him how to take some of that follow-up work off your plate?"*

## 3. The Script (Once Doug answers)

### Phase 1: Pattern Interrupt & Permission
> *"Hey Doug, it's Michael Anyanwu. I know I'm catching you in the middle of your day — do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I'm calling, and then you can hang up if it's a waste of your time?"*

### Phase 2: The Hook (Specificity & Pain)
> *"I run a local business consultancy here in DFW. I was looking at First Texas Roofing, and I know you personally oversee a lot of the job sites and deal with adjusters. A massive problem I see with owner-operators in roofing is that when you're up on a roof or on the phone with State Farm, you miss inbound calls, and those homeowners just call the next roofer on Google."*

### Phase 3: The Pivot (No-Oriented Question)
> *"Would it be a ridiculous idea to ask what your system is right now for capturing those inbound leads when your hands are full?"*

## 4. High-Yield Discovery Questions
*(If he opens up, ask these to widen the pain and surface the cost of inaction)*
1. *"When hail hits and your phone starts ringing, what happens if you're stuck on a job site and can't answer?"*
2. *"How much of your evening is spent returning voicemails only to find out they already hired someone else?"*
3. *"If you could instantly engage a missed call via text and get their address before they call a competitor, what would that do for your close rate?"*

## 5. Objection Handling (Acknowledge & Pivot)

| Objection | The Play |
|---|---|
| **"I always answer my phone."** | *"That’s impressive, and it's probably why you get good reviews. But what happens when you're negotiating with an adjuster for 45 minutes? Does it ever feel like you have to choose between serving a current client and capturing a new one?"* |
| **"I'm not interested in buying software."** | *"I completely understand. I don't sell software subscriptions. I map out workflow bottlenecks for local owners. How well is your current setup handling follow-ups with people waiting on insurance checks?"* |
| **"Send me an email."** | *"Happy to. So I don't send you a generic link, what's your biggest headache right now: missing calls while on a roof, or chasing down adjusters?"* |

## 6. The Close (Zero-Risk Diagnostic)
> *"I’d love to show you a 5-minute visual map of how a similar contractor automated their missed-call capture. The worst that happens is you get a free map of your workflow to keep. Do you have 15 minutes on [Day]?"*
