# 📞 Follow-Up Call Battlecard: Mark Leal (State Farm)

**Goal:** Secure a 15-minute workflow mapping session.

---

## 1. The Context (10-Second Review)
- **Who:** Mark Leal, Agency Owner
- **Company:** State Farm (Arlington & Euless)
- **Target Demo:** Multi-location Insurance Agency. High-volume producer.
- **The Angle:** "The 2-Office Dilemma." He has staff fielding service calls across two locations. Managing lead routing and ensuring staff input data into the mandatory State Farm corporate CRM (ECRM) perfectly is a massive pain point.
- **Current Status:** You called yesterday (Sept 23) and hit the gatekeeper (Lisa). She said call volume was manageable and gave you his email. You opted NOT to email (spam risk) and are making a second call attempt today to try and bypass/pivot.

## 2. Gatekeeper Strategy 
*(If you hit Lisa or another staff member again)*
- **The Ask:** *"Hey Lisa, it's Michael from church again. Is Mark in the office today, or is he over at the other location?"*
- **The Pivot (If he's out/busy):** *"No worries. Since I'm working with a few other local owners on automating their repetitive service tasks, I wanted to ask—when you guys get slammed at the Arlington office, how frustrating is it to make sure leads get passed to Euless without falling through the cracks or getting lost in ECRM?"*
- **The Ask for Access:** *"I'm building a system to take that exact manual data entry off your plate so you guys can just focus on writing policies. What's the best time for me to catch him so I can show him how that works for you all?"*

## 3. The Script

### Phase 1: The Warm "Church" Pattern Interrupt
> *"Hey Mark, it's Michael. We actually go to the same church. I know you're busy running two offices—do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I'm calling, and then you can hang up if it's not relevant?"*

### Phase 2: The Hook (Specificity & Pain)
> *"I run an AI and automation consultancy here locally. A massive problem I see with multi-location agencies is that staff get completely bogged down handling routine service and billing calls. That means they spend hours doing customer support instead of actually quoting new auto and home policies."*

### Phase 3: The Pivot (No-Oriented Question)
> *"Would it be a ridiculous idea to ask how you're currently keeping your team out of the service-call weeds so they can focus on writing new business?"*

## 4. High-Yield Discovery Questions
1. *"When Arlington is slammed, what is your exact system for routing a new inbound quote request to Euless without it hitting voicemail?"*
2. *"How much of your team's day is spent manually logging notes into the corporate ECRM instead of talking to prospects?"*
3. *"If you could automate the repetitive policy and billing questions so your staff never had to answer them, how many more policies do you think they could write in a month?"*

## 5. Objection Handling (Acknowledge & Pivot)

| Objection | The Play |
|---|---|
| **"State Farm corporate forces us to use their systems."** | *"I completely understand. Corporate compliance is strict. I don't replace ECRM—I build the 'glue' that automatically logs the data into it so your staff doesn't have to do it manually. How much do your reps hate doing that data entry right now?"* |
| **"We are good on call volume / fully staffed."** | *"That’s awesome that you have a solid team. Usually, top producers are terrible at admin. If they have capacity, I build the systems that handle the busywork so they can spend 100% of their time closing deals. Would it be a terrible idea to see what that looks like?"* |
| **"Send me an email."** | *"Happy to. So I don't send you generic spam, what is your biggest headache right now: staff doing too much customer service, or lead leakage between your two offices?"* |

## 6. The Close (Zero-Risk Diagnostic)
> *"I’d love to show you a 5-minute visual map of how an automated lead-routing and triage pipeline works for a multi-location agency. The worst that happens is you get a free map of the workflow to keep. Do you have 15 minutes on [Day]?"*
