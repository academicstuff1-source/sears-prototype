# 🎯 Account Intelligence: Rapid Relief Mobile IV

**Target:** Wes (Founder) and John (Father) | **Industry:** Mobile IV & Wellness | **Est. Size:** Solo/Duo Operator

---

## 1. Operational Snapshot
Rapid Relief Mobile IV is a true father-and-son operation in DFW. Wes (the founder) is a former firefighter and paramedic. This means he has deep clinical and emergency experience, giving him high credibility, but he is fundamentally an operator, not a back-office administrator.

## 2. Tech & Friction Signals
- **The "Dirty Hands" Dilemma (Clinical Edition):** In the mobile IV business, you are driving to a client's house or hotel, prepping a sterile field, and inserting an IV line. During a 45-minute appointment, both of Wes's hands are literally tied up. He cannot answer the phone.
- **Speed to Lead:** IV hydration is highly impulsive and urgent (e.g., severe hangover, sudden flu symptoms, athletic recovery). If a prospect calls and it goes to voicemail, they will instantly Google the next mobile IV service. 

## 3. High-Probability Pain Points
1. **[Missed Calls = Missed Revenue]:** The average IV drip is $150-$300. Missing two calls a week while with other patients costs them thousands of dollars a month.
2. **[Manual Intake/Consents]:** Sending medical history forms and consent waivers manually via email or relying on patients to fill them out on a clipboard when they arrive is a massive time sink.
3. **[Routing & Logistics]:** Constantly texting back and forth to figure out patient addresses and ETA updates while driving across DFW.

## 4. Key Decision Makers
- **Wes** — Founder (Primary target, likely the one in the field)

## 5. Recommended Strategic Angles
- **Angle A (The Missed Call):** "Wes, as a former paramedic, I know you're hands-on with patients. When you're in the middle of dropping a line for a client, what happens to the inbound calls you get for new bookings?"
- **Angle B (Pre-Med Credibility):** "I'm a pre-med student building workflow automation for independent clinics. I noticed you're running a mobile operation, which usually means patient intake and consent forms are a logistical nightmare when you're on the road."
